Problem Statement

Field observations indicated premature shutdown near a displayed 20% SOC and gauge instability during abrupt acceleration. The engineering diagnosis considered pack-level estimation, cell dispersion, load transients, and production quality together rather than treating the issue as a single isolated fault.

Premature shutdowns

Voltage sag under load could trigger protective behavior before the displayed energy estimate reached zero.

SOC instability

Rapid current changes could expose limitations in a gauge that relied on insufficiently dynamic correction.

Cell heterogeneity

Capacity, internal resistance, and assembly variation could cause the weakest cell group to constrain usable pack energy.

LFP estimation difficulty

The flat LFP OCV-SOC region makes voltage-only estimation less informative across a large part of the operating range.

Engineering Approach

The proposed approach linked field diagnosis, battery quality, model-based estimation, embedded electronics, and production supervision into one engineering workflow.

Battery quality procedure

Cell qualification, matching, assembly checks, and traceability were structured to help limit pack variability.

Modeling & estimation

An ECM, UKF, and learned residual correction were combined to support more robust SOC estimation.

Embedded BMS architecture

A proposed STM32-based architecture linked measurement, protection, balancing, communication, and supervision.

Industrial supervision

Pack and production data were organized for monitoring, diagnosis, quality control, and KPI analysis.

Embedded Hardware Design

The hardware work defined a proposed 16-cell BMS architecture and PCB design that could support measurement, processing, communication, balancing, and protection functions.

Battery monitoring front-end

Cell-voltage and temperature acquisition, input filtering, measurement isolation, and diagnostic access.

STM32 processing unit

Real-time acquisition, estimator execution, state supervision, fault handling, and communication management.

Power stage

High-side switching, pre-charge control, current-path monitoring, and a bidirectional active-balancing concept.

Communication

CAN for vehicle integration, with I2C and SPI interfaces for sensing, memory, and peripheral devices.

Protection and EMC

Input protection, controlled grounding, separation of sensitive measurement paths, transient suppression, and layout practices intended to improve electromagnetic robustness.

Validation Strategy

Prototype-level

Acquisition and control paths were exercised with prototype instrumentation to check measurement flow and embedded integration assumptions.

Results and Impact

The project produced an integrated engineering proposal and a staged validation path. The outcomes below describe intended and prototype-supported impact without implying production validation.

More reliable SOC estimation

The hybrid estimation chain supports more robust correction across dynamic load conditions.

Reduced premature cut-offs

Better estimation and pack supervision can help reduce avoidable protective shutdowns.

Improved usable energy

More representative SOC information can help operators use available pack energy within configured limits.

Better battery quality control

Qualification and traceability procedures make cell and pack variation easier to identify and manage.

Industrial scalability

The proposed architecture separates measurement, estimation, supervision, and data services so each layer can be developed and validated incrementally.

Engineering Lessons Learned

The work reinforced that battery reliability is a system problem: estimation quality, electrical design, cell consistency, software supervision, and production discipline influence one another.

Model and data must agree

A physical model gives structure, while measured residuals reveal behavior that still needs correction.

The weakest cell shapes pack behavior

Pack-level energy availability depends on cell matching, resistance dispersion, and assembly quality.

Validation must be staged

Simulation and prototype tests reduce uncertainty, but they do not replace full real-time validation on the target pack.

Industrialization starts with traceability

A repeatable BMS process needs controlled data, clear quality checks, and diagnosable production records.
