Initial Challenge

Automating the hot nut insertion process faced hurdles due to an uncertain optimal cycle time ($t_{\text{cycle}}$) and the risk of polycarbonate thermal damage. The initial theoretical estimate, based on simplifying assumptions, required validation. This study aimed to replace the estimate with high-fidelity simulation data for process reliability and optimization.

Objective: Validating Heating Time

The primary objective was to quantify the accuracy of the initial $\mathbf{4.22\text{ s}}$ theoretical heating time derived from the Lumped Capacitance Method (LCM). This involved using high-fidelity Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to simulate transient thermal behavior realistically, establishing a precise, validated heating duration to reach the target $170^\circ\text{C}$, and enabling a reliable control strategy.

A. The LCM Flaw

The LCM calculation relied on a single, estimated $h_{\text{eff}}$ and ignored crucial 3D geometry (grooves, stack). This led to an inaccurate $t_{\text{LCM}}$ of $\mathbf{4.22 \text{ s}}$.

B. The Comparison: LCM vs. FEA

Heating curves reveal the discrepancy: the validated ANSYS FEA model shows significantly faster heating, reaching target $\approx$ 28.7% quicker than predicted.

C. The Physical Cause

FEA identifies the reason: significant axial heat conduction between nuts acts as a secondary heat path, accelerating the process beyond LCM's convective assumptions.

Conclusion & Engineering Impact

High-fidelity FEA conclusively demonstrated the initial theoretical heating time estimate was highly conservative. This finding unlocked a significant opportunity for process optimization via a much faster, validated cycle time.

Time to Reach $170^\circ\text{C}$ $4.22\text{ s}$ $\mathbf{3.28\text{ s}}$ +28.7% (Overestimated)

Final Engineering Strategy

  • Productivity Gain: The validated heating time ($\mathbf{3.28\text{ s}}$) is substantially faster than the production cadence ($\mathbf{15-30\text{ s}}$). The heating step is confirmed not the process bottleneck, enabling focus on optimizing other cycle stages.
  • Simplified Control & Safety: Polycarbonate thermal damage is effectively managed by limiting source temperature ($T_{\infty} = 180^\circ\text{C}$). The analysis guarantees nut thermal stability well within cycle time, simplifying control logic and ensuring material integrity without complex time-based cutoffs.
