Transient Thermal Analysis for Process Optimization: A Comparative Study of Lumped Capacitance Modeling and Finite Element Simulation

Automated Hot Nut Insertion in Polycarbonate Components

Rafi NASRALAH, ENSET Mohammedia (Building on SMCV Internship Experience)

Download Full Study Report (PDF)

The Core Problem

The drive to automate the hot nut insertion process was significantly challenged by uncertainty around the optimal heating cycle time ($t_{\text{cycle}}$). An inaccurate estimate risked either incomplete heating (poor joint quality) or overheating (thermal damage to the polycarbonate casing). The initial theoretical calculation needed rigorous validation.

Study Objective

This study aimed to validate the initial theoretical heating time ($4.22\text{ s}$) derived from the Lumped Capacitance Method (LCM). The goal was to replace this estimate with a precise value obtained through high-fidelity Finite Element Analysis (FEA), thereby enabling a reliable control strategy and paving the way for process optimization. The target temperature for successful insertion was $170^\circ\text{C}$.

Comparative Analysis: Theory vs. Simulation

Method 1: Lumped Capacitance Model (LCM)

A simplified theoretical approach assuming uniform internal temperature. Relied on an estimated effective heat transfer coefficient ($h_{\text{eff}}$) and neglected 3D geometry (nut grooves, stacking effects).

Result: $t_{\text{LCM}} = 4.22\text{ s}$

Method 2: Finite Element Analysis (FEA)

A high-fidelity numerical simulation (ANSYS Mechanical) accounting for transient heat transfer, actual 3D geometry, material properties, and contact thermal resistance between nuts.

Result: $t_{\text{ANSYS}} = \mathbf{3.28\text{ s}}$

Key Finding: The Discrepancy

The FEA revealed significantly faster heating ($\approx$ 28.7% quicker) than the LCM predicted. Analysis of heat flux vectors (see gallery) confirmed that axial heat conduction between stacked nuts provided a major secondary heat path, a factor ignored by the simplified LCM.

Engineering Impact & Final Strategy

  • Productivity Gain: The validated heating time ($\mathbf{3.28\text{ s}}$) is substantially faster than the overall production cadence ($\mathbf{15-30\text{ s}}$), confirming heating is not the process bottleneck.
  • Simplified Control & Safety: Thermal damage risk is mitigated by simply limiting the source temperature ($T_{\infty} = 180^\circ\text{C}$). The analysis ensures the nut reaches thermal stability safely within the required cycle time, eliminating the need for complex, time-based heating controls.

Next Steps: Thermomechanical Analysis

With the thermal profile validated, the next crucial phase involves a Coupled Thermomechanical Analysis. Using the final temperature distribution from this study ($T$ at $3.28\text{ s}$) as the initial condition, this analysis will simulate the physical insertion process. The goals are to determine the optimal Insertion Force ($F_{\text{insert}}$) required and to predict the final Joint Integrity (pull-out strength), considering polycarbonate viscoplasticity and large deformation mechanics.
