1. PLC and HMI Programming

**Project Focus:** Development of the full functional cycle and advanced control features for the injection machine, emphasizing safety and operational flexibility.

Technical Details:

  • **Hardware/Software:** Siemens **TIA Portal V19** used to program a **S7-1200 PLC**.
  • **Core Achievement (Functional Cycle):** Implemented the full machine cycle logic, incorporating essential **safety features** (Emergency Stop, pressure limit detection, material presence detection, etc.).
  • **Advanced Control:** Developed and implemented a **PID control loop** for precise temperature regulation.
  • **Modularity and HMI Interfacing:** The code is **fully modular**. HMI communications allow operators to **modify all critical parameters** (temperature setpoint, injection time, maintaining time, cooling time, etc.) in real-time.
  • **State Visualization:** The HMI provides clear **visible representation** of the current state of the cycle (e.g., mold opening, mold closing, cooling).

2. Mechanical Insertion Arm Design

**Context:** In the production workshop, certain plastic parts require nut insertion before final assembly. This operation was previously performed using a pneumatic press with suboptimal temperature control settings and insufficient nut fixture support.

Problem Statement & Impact

  • The previous manual correction of poorly inserted nuts led to **variability in parts quality**.
  • The process resulted in **time loss** and a **reduction in overall process efficiency**.

**Objective:** The goal of this phase was to design a manual insertion arm capable of executing this task reliably, repeatedly, and seamlessly integrated into the production cycle, thereby ensuring consistent quality and improving workshop productivity.

Results and Next Step Decision

The implementation of the mechanical insertion arm significantly reduced nut perpendicularity defects and improved the operator's working conditions. However, this solution presented a major limitation: **insertion remained manual and sequential, nut by nut, which slowed down the production rate.**

**Decision:** In the studied industrial context, it is imperative to guarantee both part quality and high productivity. Consequently, despite the mechanical arm being an improvement over the initial press, it did not fully meet the production line requirements. It was therefore decided to develop a more efficient, automated solution capable of ensuring the **simultaneous insertion of all nuts** in each plastic casing (leading to Section 3).

3. Nut Insertion Press Design and Programming

**Objective:** Following the conclusions of the mechanical arm project, this phase involved developing a high-performance, automated solution to ensure the simultaneous and reliable insertion of all necessary nuts in the plastic component, reconciling quality and high production rates.

Technical Scope:

  • **Mechanical Design:** Complete structural and functional design of the press (using Autodesk Inventor).
  • **Automation:** PLC programming (TIA Portal) for sequence control, safety, and operational logic.
  • **HMI Development:** Creation of the Human-Machine Interface for command, monitoring, and parameter setting.
  • **Component Sizing:** Detailed study and dimensioning of **sensors**, **actuators**, and **pre-actuators** (pneumatic, electrical).
  • **Thermal Analysis:** Execution of a **thermal study** for the heating process to ensure optimal and safe nut insertion temperatures.

4. Industrial IT Maintenance

**Context and Justification:** IT workstations, running the industrial software Inhemeter, are central to the configuration, parameterization, and testing of electricity meters. Any material or software failure can cause production delays or shutdowns, highlighting the strategic dimension of IT maintenance to ensure continuous availability and performance.

Diagnosis and Troubleshooting:

Configuration workstations were experiencing significant instability, including slow performance and frequent crashes during parameterization. The root cause was identified as an unstable, Chinese-language Virtual Machine (VM) running Windows XP (required for the Inhemeter software) on newer hardware.

Actions Taken:
  • Installed the latest stable and professional version of the Virtual Machine software with an **English interface**.
  • Reinstalled and configured **Windows XP** within the new VM instance.
  • Updated and installed necessary drivers to ensure compatibility with the industrial software and peripherals.
  • Optimized VM settings to improve overall system speed and stability.

Results and Standardization Decision

These maintenance actions immediately restored the proper functioning of the configuration workstations, significantly reducing crashes and improving the fluidity of the meter programming process.

**Generalization:** Based on these results, it was decided to generalize the new software configuration across all affected workstations and adopt a standardized IT maintenance procedure to ensure the durability of the improvements and prevent recurrence of the issues.

General Conclusion and Perspectives

During this internship, I participated in several major projects aimed primarily at the improvement and automation of the nut insertion processes in electricity meters. Specifically, I:

These missions highlighted the importance of planning, technical precision, and the integration of mechanical and electrical systems in a real industrial environment. The overall assessment of the internship is therefore highly positive, both technically and in terms of project organization and management.
